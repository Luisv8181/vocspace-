# Accommodation Foundry

## Purpose

VocSpace can eventually move beyond recommending accommodations and become a place where accommodations are **designed, simulated, prototyped, tested, and refined**.

The long-term loop is:

**Need → Design → Simulate → Build → Test → Adapt**

This extends the VocSpace model from accessibility intelligence into an accommodation innovation laboratory.

## Accommodation Compiler

A core research concept is the **Accommodation Compiler**:

**Human Need**
→ **Functional Barrier**
→ **Accommodation Pattern**
→ **Interaction / Mechanical Design**
→ **Technical Specification**
→ **Simulation**
→ **Prototype**
→ **Validation**

The compiler should preserve the reasoning behind each design decision rather than outputting a black-box answer.

## Design inputs

A future Foundry workflow may capture:

- Person's goal
- Task
- Functional demands
- Barrier
- Context/environment
- Strengths and existing strategies
- Physical constraints
- Cognitive or sensory requirements
- Available materials
- Available technology
- Budget
- Portability requirements
- Durability requirements
- Safety constraints
- Implementation setting

## Simulation before fabrication

Simulation can help answer design questions before building a physical prototype.

Potential applications include:

- Dimensions and fit
- Range of motion
- Reachability
- Force and torque
- Stability
- Collision detection
- Mechanical clearance
- Weight distribution
- Material assumptions
- Repeated-use scenarios
- Failure points
- Environmental constraints

Tools such as Blender, CAD systems, physics engines, computer vision, and procedural generation can become part of the development workflow.

Simulation is a design aid. It does not by itself establish clinical effectiveness, structural safety, product safety, or regulatory compliance.

## Validation

Every prototype should have an explicit validation plan.

A validation cycle may include:

1. Define the intended participation goal.
2. Define measurable functional requirements.
3. Create the prototype or simulation.
4. Test the prototype against the requirements.
5. Conduct structured user or stakeholder testing when appropriate.
6. Record failures, tradeoffs, and unintended effects.
7. Revise the design.
8. Compare outcomes against the original barrier.

A successful accommodation is not merely an elegant object or clever interface. It is a solution that improves meaningful participation under the relevant real-world constraints.

## Safety and professional boundaries

VocSpace should never imply that an AI-generated design is automatically safe to use.

High-risk physical designs, load-bearing devices, mobility equipment, medical devices, electrical systems, and other safety-critical artifacts require appropriate engineering, clinical, regulatory, and real-world review.

The Foundry should distinguish:

- Concept
- Simulation result
- Prototype
- Tested prototype
- Validated intervention
- Production-ready artifact

These states should never be conflated.

## Research direction

The Accommodation Foundry creates a bridge among:

**Clinical Rehabilitation Counseling + Accessibility + AI + HCI + CAD + 3D Printing + Simulation + Sensors + Robotics**

The CRC contribution remains fundamental. The starting point is not the technology. The starting point is the person's goal, functional experience, environmental demand, and desired participation outcome.

## Long-term vision

A future VocSpace interaction could be as simple as:

> "Design an accommodation for this barrier and show me several ways it could work."

VocSpace could then generate candidate concepts, explain why they may fit the functional need, simulate them where possible, produce prototype specifications, create fabrication-ready artifacts where appropriate, and establish a validation protocol.

The goal is not to automate judgment.

The goal is to make accessibility innovation dramatically easier to explore, prototype, and learn from.

## Guiding principle

> **Design the accommodation. Simulate the idea. Build the prototype. Test the outcome. Learn from the person.**
